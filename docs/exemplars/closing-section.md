# Where the Four Domains Meet

The four domains are not four subjects. They are four views of one chain of decisions, and the exam tests the chain.

Domain I installs the vocabulary and the discipline: what an AI system is, what can go wrong, which characteristic of AI makes that failure possible, who owns the decision, and where it is written down. Domain II attaches legal duties to those decisions, and the duties divide by role, so an organization can be a developer, a provider, a deployer and a controller at the same time and owe a different thing under each. Domains III and IV run the chain, first through the build and then through adoption and operation, and each link produces a record that the next link depends on.

Three questions recur in every domain, and they are the plain-language form of what the BOK asks:

- **Who owns this decision?** Accountability is a named human, it is never delegable to a model or a vendor, and the person who runs governance is not the person who owns the systems.
- **What could go wrong, and for whom?** The sort decides the control and the owner. A harm that is not assigned to a category tends not to get one.
- **Where is it written down?** Governance that stops at approval ends up describing a system that no longer exists, because AI systems change underneath the documents written about them.

Most scenario items are one link of that chain, and the most common trap is a distractor that sounds prudent but belongs to another link. A contract term, an incident response and a monitoring threshold are all reasonable answers to "what should the organization do," and only one of them is the current link. Read the stem for where in the chain the organization actually stands.

Two habits make the domains cohere. Learn the **pairings**, because most applied questions are a pairing with half missing: a characteristic and its control, a legal role and its duty, a risk and its owner, a documentation artifact and the decision it evidences. And keep **status** in view, because the EU AI Act in particular now exists in two versions, and the difference between adopted, in force and applies from is itself testable.

## Preparing for the exam

The book covers the material in order. The rest of the app is built for the work around it, and the two are meant to be used together.

1. **Read this book straight through once.** The material accumulates, and later chapters assume the earlier vocabulary.
2. **Work the Mind Map.** Open a section, read the node's notes in the drawer, then open its children to go deeper. Each node has buttons that filter the Quiz and the Flashcards to that node, and every question and card is tagged down to a key term, so completing all of a node's leaves turns it green by definition. The goal is a fully green map.
3. **Drill the weak nodes.** Nodes colour by performance: grey untested, then red, amber and green. Re-drill whatever is still red before moving on.
4. **Sit timed exams.** The Test tab offers a full exam of 100 questions in three hours and a practice quiz of 25 in one hour, drawn to the blueprint's domain weights, with multi-select questions scored all-or-nothing as on the real exam. Answers are revealed only on submission.

Consistent scores above 85% under exam conditions are a reasonable signal that you are ready.

One last caution. The exam is written against the BOK, and this book follows BOK v2.1 with the law as it stands in September 2026. Both move. When a fact in this book turns on a date, a status or a question range, check it against the current Body of Knowledge before you rely on it.
