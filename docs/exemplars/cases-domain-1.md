# Domain I Case Library: Verified Real-World AI Governance Cases

Worked examples for Domain I (Foundations) of the IAPP AIGP Body of Knowledge. Each case was fetched from the source listed in its Verified source URL column during preparation, and the details below are drawn from that source. Cases are ordered to walk the competency areas of Chapters 2 and 3: expectations and literacy (I.B), cross-functional and board oversight (I.B), and policy, oversight and third-party risk across the life cycle (I.C).

| Case | Citation | Date | Verified source URL | BOK competency it illustrates | What it shows (2-4 sentences) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| EU AI Act Article 4 (AI literacy) | EU AI Act, Article 4 (AI literacy), in force 2 February 2025; restated by the Digital Omnibus on AI with effect from 27 July 2026 | 2 February 2025, amended 27 July 2026 | https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-4 | I.B, I.C | Article 4 places a legal duty on providers and deployers to make sure the people who operate AI systems have enough AI literacy for their role. It makes workforce training an obligation rather than a good practice. The Digital Omnibus restated the duty as taking measures that support literacy rather than guaranteeing a level, which is the model for a training duty scaled to what an organization can actually control. |
| Samsung generative AI ban | Samsung restricts staff use of public generative AI tools after sensitive internal data was pasted into them; reported by Bloomberg, Forbes and The Straits Times | 2 May 2023 | https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2023-05-02/samsung-bans-chatgpt-and-other-generative-ai-use-by-staff-after-leak | I.B, I.C | After employees leaked internal material to a public chatbot, Samsung restricted staff use of generative AI on company networks and devices and described it as temporary while it built a secure alternative. It is the standard shadow AI event, where the cause is an unapproved path rather than a hostile insider. The lesson is that a restriction on its own does not remove the behavior, so the durable control pairs the rule with a sanctioned tool. |
| Drivers v. Uber and Ola | ECLI:NL:GHAMS:2023:793, Amsterdam Court of Appeal, 4 April 2023; non-compliance ruling of 5 October 2023 ordering EUR 584,000 in penalty payments | 4 April 2023 | https://apps.eurofound.europa.eu/platformeconomydb/amsterdam-court-of-appeal-rules-uber-violated-data-protection-rights-110150 | I.C | The court found that Uber and Ola breached drivers' data protection rights in the automated deactivation of driver accounts, a case known as robo-firing. It rejected Uber's attempt to rely on a human in the loop, because the human role was not meaningful. A later ruling ordered Uber to pay EUR 584,000 for failing to comply. The pair of decisions is the authority that a nominal human presence does not satisfy an oversight duty. |
| Robodebt Royal Commission | Royal Commission into the Robodebt Scheme, final report tabled 7 July 2023 | 7 July 2023 | https://robodebt.royalcommission.gov.au/publications/report | I.C | The Royal Commission examined an automated income-averaging and debt-raising program that issued welfare debts to a large population, many of them wrong. It found the scheme unlawful and traced the harm to governance: those who approved and ran it did not test whether it was valid, and the human review was ritual rather than real. It is the public-sector case of oversight failure at program scale. |
| Bauserman v. Michigan (MiDAS) | Bauserman v. State of Michigan Unemployment Insurance Agency, class settlement finally approved 29 January 2024; USD 20 million | 30 January 2024 | https://www.michigan.gov/ag/news/press-releases/2024/01/30/class-action-settlement-approved-by-court-of-claims | I.C | Michigan's MiDAS system automatically accused roughly 3,000 unemployment claimants of fraud and seized money and assets, with little effective route to challenge the result. The state settled the class action for USD 20 million. The case shows what happens when an automated determination is treated as final and the avenue to contest it was never designed. |
| EEOC v. iTutorGroup | EEOC v. iTutorGroup, Inc., et al., Civil Action No. 1:22-cv-02565; consent decree announced 11 September 2023; USD 365,000 | 11 September 2023 | https://www.eeoc.gov/newsroom/itutorgroup-pay-365000-settle-eeoc-discriminatory-hiring-suit | I.C, I.B | The tutoring company set its applicant software to reject women aged 55 and older and men aged 60 and older automatically, and turned away more than 200 US-based applicants. It settled under the Age Discrimination in Employment Act. The employer that wrote the rule answered for the screening its software performed, which is the accountability point and the reason a named owner has to sit on the design of a hiring rule. |
| FTC v. Evolv Technologies | FTC v. Evolv Technologies Holdings, Inc., complaint and stipulated order, US District Court for the District of Massachusetts; FTC press release 26 November 2024 | 26 November 2024 | https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/11/ftc-takes-action-against-evolv-technologies-deceiving-users-about-its-ai-powered-security-screening | I.C | The FTC alleged that Evolv overstated what its AI weapons screening system could detect, while schools buying it were locked into multi-year contracts. The proposed order bars unsupported AI claims and lets affected K-12 customers cancel. It is a supply-chain case where the harm landed on the deployers, because schools relied on the vendor's performance claims for the safety of children. |
| Garante v. DeepSeek | Garante per la protezione dei dati personali, urgent limitation order of 30 January 2025, doc. web 10097450 | 30 January 2025 | https://www.garanteprivacy.it/home/docweb/-/docweb-display/docweb/10097450 | I.C | The Italian authority ordered an immediate limitation on the processing of Italian users' data by the companies behind the DeepSeek chatbot, after judging their response insufficient; they had argued that EU law did not apply to them. It is a regulator action against a model provider that reached every downstream deployer and user in Italy at once. It shows that model supply is a governance dependency, not only a procurement choice. |
| Dutch DPA v. Clearview AI | Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens decision of 16 May 2024, published 3 September 2024; EUR 30.5 million fine plus a penalty of up to EUR 5 million | 3 September 2024 | https://www.autoriteitpersoonsgegevens.nl/en/current/dutch-dpa-imposes-a-fine-on-clearview-because-of-illegal-data-collection-for-facial-recognition | I.C | The Dutch authority fined Clearview AI for building a facial recognition database without a legal basis and for failing its transparency and access duties, including for its law enforcement and public defenders service. A penalty for continuing non-compliance attached to the order. It shows that the duties run to the provider of the AI system, and that a deployer who uses an unlawful provider's output is exposed to the same unlawfulness. |
| SEC v. Delphia and Global Predictions | In the Matter of Delphia (USA) Inc. and In the Matter of Global Predictions Inc., SEC administrative orders, 18 March 2024; USD 400,000 combined civil penalties | 18 March 2024 | https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2024-36 | I.B | The SEC charged two investment advisers with making false and misleading statements about their use of AI and they paid USD 400,000 in combined penalties. It is an early AI-washing enforcement, and it makes executives answerable for what the organization claims about its AI whether or not the technology performs. It is the enforcement reason a claim about an AI system has to be evidenced. |
| In re Boeing Company Derivative Litigation | In re The Boeing Company Derivative Litigation, C.A. No. 2019-0907-MTZ (Del. Ch. 7 September 2021) | 7 September 2021 | https://law.justia.com/cases/delaware/court-of-chancery/2021/c-a-no-2019-0907-mtz-0.html | I.B | Shareholders sued Boeing's directors for oversight failures after the 737 MAX crashes, and the Court of Chancery let the Caremark claims proceed past a motion to dismiss. The court reasoned that the board had not built a reporting system that carried safety information up to it. Boeing is the practical warning that a board must receive the right information, not merely hold the right meetings, and that is the same duty an AI oversight body serves. |
| In re Caremark International Inc. Derivative Litigation | In re Caremark International Inc. Derivative Litigation, 698 A.2d 959 (Del. Ch. 1996), decided 25 September 1996 | 25 September 1996 | https://courtlistener.com/opinion/1968607/in-re-caremark-international-inc-derivative-litigation/ | I.B | Caremark set the Delaware standard for director oversight: liability requires a sustained or systematic failure to exercise oversight, such as an utter failure to attempt to assure a reasonable information and reporting system. It is the doctrinal source of the idea that a board's duty includes an information system for the risks it oversees. For AI, it is the authority behind giving the board a channel for AI risk rather than a one-off briefing. |
| SAP Global AI ethics governance | SAP Global AI Ethics Policy, Global AI Ethics Steering Committee and Global AI Ethics Advisory Panel, coordinated by the AI Ethics Office; SAP AI Ethics Handbook, 2023 | Handbook 2023; page current to 2026 | https://www.sap.com/products/artificial-intelligence/ai-ethics.html | I.B | SAP documents an internal Global AI Ethics Steering Committee of senior leaders drawn from different functions, an external advisory panel, and an AI Ethics Office that coordinates both under a single policy. The committee reviews the company's approach, processes and product capabilities. It is a working example of the steering layer, a cross-functional body with a defined remit, rather than one owner holding the whole subject. |
| National Eating Disorders Association (Tessa) | National Eating Disorders Association suspends its Tessa chatbot after harmful dieting advice; BBC News, 31 May 2023 | 31 May 2023 | https://www.bbc.com/news/world-us-canada-65771872 | I.C | A small nonprofit in the eating-disorder sector put a chatbot in front of vulnerable users after closing its live helpline, and withdrew it when users reported advice that promoted calorie restriction and dieting. The association had neither the clinical review nor the monitoring the use case required. It is the size and sector cautionary case: controls have to match the risk of the population served, and a small organization needs a scaled-down version of them, not none. |

## UNVERIFIED, excluded

The following leads were checked and are kept out of the main table. Each is listed with the reason.

| Case | Why it is excluded |
|---|---|
| SEC v. SolarWinds Corporation and Timothy G. Brown | Not usable as a standing board or executive oversight authority. The SEC brought fraud and internal control charges in October 2023, but dismissed its remaining claims against SolarWinds with prejudice on 20 November 2025, so the matter ended without a sustained finding. Sources reviewed: SEC litigation releases LR-25887 and LR-26423, and the Harvard Law School Forum on Corporate Governance post of 7 December 2025. |
| Marchand v. Barnhill (Blue Bell Creameries), 212 A.3d 805 (Del. 2019) | Disposition not confirmed from the opinion text in this session. The CourtListener PDF was located but only partially extracted, so the Caremark holding was not read in full and the case is left out rather than cited on a secondary summary alone. |

DoNotPay, where a small AI startup paid USD 193,000 over deceptive robot lawyer claims (https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/02/ftc-finalizes-order-donotpay-prohibits-deceptive-ai-lawyer-claims-imposes-monetary-relief-requires).
