# AIGP Exam Question Bank

Curated review questions organized by knowledge area, with answers and explanations.

## AI and AI Governance Review Questions

### Q1.1: The Netflix Recommendation Engine and Google Search Autocomplete are examples of

- **A.** AI agent
- **B.** General AI
- **C.** Narrow AI
- **D.** Training techniques

> **Answer: C** — Narrow AI includes AI systems that perform highly focused tasks. Other examples are spam filters, license plate recognition systems, and Apple Face ID.

---

### Q1.2: An organization has developed an AI system that receives and analyzes input from sensors and takes action as needed. This system is an example of

- **A.** AI agent
- **B.** Robot
- **C.** General AI
- **D.** Operational technology

> **Answer: A** — An AI agent is a system that receives input from systems and sensors and takes action to achieve specific goals.

---

### Q1.3: An EU-based retail organization retains data about individual transactions and maintains databases with detailed customer information. The organization wants to build an AI system to analyze its customers and transactions, to help better understand sales history, and to develop better sales and marketing strategies. What interaction, if any, is required with customers before launching this project?

- **A.** Customers can opt out of this activity.
- **B.** Customers must be notified of this activity.
- **C.** No customer interaction is required.
- **D.** Customers must explicitly consent to this activity.

> **Answer: D** — According to the GDPR, organizations must obtain explicit consent from customers before their information can be used by the AI system for this (or any other) purpose.

---

### Q1.4: An IT department is building an AI system and will begin training it with labeled data. What kind of training is this?

- **A.** Supervised learning
- **B.** Unsupervised learning
- **C.** Semi-supervised learning
- **D.** Reinforcement learning

> **Answer: A** — Training an AI system with labeled data is known as supervised learning.

---

### Q1.5: A regional bank is building an AI system to help analyze loan applications and make approval and interest rate recommendations. What kind of harm must the bank ensure is not realized?

- **A.** Financial
- **B.** Social cohesion
- **C.** Privacy
- **D.** Bias

> **Answer: D** — The type of harm that the bank must watch for is bias, where individuals of certain demographics may be denied loans or favorable loan terms.

---

### Q1.6: A regional bank is building an AI system to help analyze loan applications and make approval and interest rate recommendations. What safeguard should the bank implement to ensure fairness?

- **A.** Event detection
- **B.** Supervised learning
- **C.** Human in the loop
- **D.** Hallucination detection

> **Answer: C** — The bank should implement a human in the loop to review loan applications and the AI system’s recommendations to ensure that the AI system is producing correct outputs.

---

### Q1.7: A social media platform uses an AI algorithm to select items to appear in users’ data feeds. What potential harm could result if the AI algorithm is biased?

- **A.** Job displacement
- **B.** Manipulation of public opinion
- **C.** Erosion of human autonomy
- **D.** Privacy

> **Answer: B** — A biased AI algorithm in a social media platform could sway public opinion.

---

### Q1.8: An organization has implemented a neural network AI system to review job applications and make interview recommendations. An employment candidate recently applied for a position, was rejected by the AI system, and requests an explanation for the rejection. What potential challenge is the organization about to face?

- **A.** Security incident
- **B.** Privacy violation
- **C.** Difficulty in providing a reason for the rejection
- **D.** Economic inequality

> **Answer: C** — Because the organization uses a neural network, known for its opacity and lack of explainability, the organization will have difficulty explaining why the AI system rejected the application.

---

### Q1.9: The highest priority in the design of the AI system controlling an autonomous vehicle should be

- **A.** Privacy
- **B.** Safety
- **C.** Fairness
- **D.** Security

> **Answer: B** — The safety of passengers and others should be the highest priority for an autonomous vehicle.

---

### Q1.10: The highest priority in the design of an AI system visually identifying returning customers in a retail store is

- **A.** Security
- **B.** Fairness
- **C.** Safety
- **D.** Privacy

> **Answer: D** — The design and operation of an AI system employing facial recognition to detect returning customers in a retail store should prioritize privacy above other considerations.

## Organizational Readiness Review Questions

### Q2.1: Which role is primarily responsible for selecting training data and conducting bias testing during model development?

- **A.** AI deployer
- **B.** End user
- **C.** AI developer
- **D.** Compliance officer

> **Answer: C** — Developers build and train models, which include curating datasets and running fairness evaluations.

---

### Q2.2: What is the most appropriate governance structure for a small startup with limited AI resources?

- **A.** Fully automated audit pipelines
- **B.** A board-level AI oversight committee
- **C.** Lightweight governance with designated role champions
- **D.** Centralized AI governance office with legal authority

> **Answer: C** — Startups typically employ simplified governance structures with a few cross-functional leads, due to their limited staff and resources.

---

### Q2.3: Which of the following best illustrates cross-functional collaboration in AI governance?

- **A.** Engineers writing code based on user feedback
- **B.** A legal team monitoring AI regulatory updates
- **C.** A data scientist leading all model deployment tasks
- **D.** An ethics lead, product manager, and ML engineer co-reviewing an AI use case

### Q2.4: Why is it important to tailor AI governance training to different stakeholder roles?

- **A.** Because only technical staff require training
- **B.** To increase automation in governance processes
- **C.** Because each role interacts with AI differently and needs context-specific knowledge
- **D.** To reduce the number of governance roles

> **Answer: C** — Training must reflect how stakeholders utilize AI to be relevant and effective.

---

### Q2.5: What distinguishes an AI user from a developer or deployer in governance terms?

- **A.** Users monitor system drift
- **B.** Users design model architectures
- **C.** Users interact with the AI in real-world scenarios and may flag harm

### Q2.6: Which governance strategy is most appropriate for a highly regulated industry like healthcare?

- **A.** Informal team agreements
- **B.** Lightweight ethics principles
- **C.** Formal reviews, documentation, and auditability

- **B.** Conducting data annotation
- **C.** Integrating models into production and setting up monitoring

- **B.** Reduced need for end-user input

### Q2.10: Which of the following would most likely appear in a well-designed AI governance training program?

- **B.** A tutorial on coding best practices

## Updating Policies for AI Review Questions

### Q3.1: Which of the following best describes a key principle of lifecycle-based AI governance?

- **A.** Apply oversight only at the deployment stage.
- **B.** Delegate AI governance solely to the data science team.
- **C.** Embed accountability mechanisms at each phase of the AI development process.
- **D.** Use governance only for models trained on sensitive data.

> **Answer: C** — AI governance must be continuous and span the entire lifecycle, from use case assessment to incident management. Limiting governance to deployment or specific data types introduces risk.

---

### Q3.2: What is the primary risk associated with using anonymized training data in AI models?

- **A.** The data cannot be used under GDPR.
- **B.** Anonymized data increases training time.
- **C.** AI models can potentially reidentify individuals through inference.
- **D.** Anonymized data always improves model fairness.

> **Answer: C** — Even anonymized data can be reverse-engineered or inferred from model behavior. This is a known risk in AI privacy and requires stronger safeguards.

---

### Q3.3: Which of the following is a valid policy control for managing third-party AI risk?

- **A.** Relying on vendor self-attestations without further review
- **B.** Requiring model documentation and training data provenance
- **C.** Limiting partnerships to vendors with ISO 42001 certification only
- **D.** Prohibiting all third-party AI tools entirely

> **Answer: B** — Governance policies should demand transparency about the model and data. Documentation helps evaluate fairness, legality, and risk exposure.

---

### Q3.4: A policy that mandates periodic monitoring for model drift is best aligned with which lifecycle stage?

- **A.** Data acquisition
- **B.** Deployment and monitoring
- **C.** Training and testing
- **D.** Use case approval

> **Answer: B** — Monitoring is a post-deployment function that ensures models continue to perform ethically and effectively under real-world conditions, for example, to detect emerging bias and drift issues.

---

### Q3.5: Which of the following is a recommended contractual clause when procuring an AI-enabled service?

- **A.** The vendor must disclose all proprietary source code.
- **B.** The vendor is exempt from bias-related complaints.
- **C.** The vendor must allow audit access and disclose data sources.
- **D.** The vendor will maintain confidentiality for three months only.

> **Answer: C** — Contracts should require transparency and auditability, especially for high-risk AI applications. This includes data origin, fairness testing, and monitoring results.

---

### Q3.6: What is one reason traditional privacy policies may be insufficient for AI systems?

- **A.** They do not address the retention of inference outputs.
- **B.** They assume all data is structured.
- **C.** They focus only on system availability.
- **D.** They prohibit all personal data processing.

> **Answer: A** — AI models may “remember” or leak training data. Traditional policies rarely account for the retention risks related to what a model has learned or memorized.

---

### Q3.7: What is the purpose of assigning risk tiers to AI vendors?

- **A.** To limit the number of contracts issued
- **B.** To determine marketing budgets for the AI solution
- **C.** To apply appropriate governance controls based on risk level
- **D.** To allow procurement to bypass risk reviews for large vendors

> **Answer: C** — Risk-based tiering ensures that higher-risk vendors (for example, those processing sensitive data or supporting critical functions) are subject to more thorough review and oversight.

---

### Q3.8: Which policy control helps enforce purpose limitation in AI pipelines?

- **A.** Allowing unlimited data reuse for internal projects
- **B.** Using a use case registry to tag approved applications of data
- **C.** Prohibiting the use of cloud storage
- **D.** Allowing only unstructured data in training

> **Answer: B** — A use case registry enables organizations to track and enforce approved purposes for data use, preventing unauthorized repurposing.

---

### Q3.9: Which of the following would most effectively reduce bias in AI model development?

- **A.** Encrypting all datasets before training
- **B.** Testing model outputs against diverse population subgroups
- **C.** Using only synthetic data in training
- **D.** Optimizing models solely for accuracy

> **Answer: B** — Fairness testing against different groups helps identify and reduce disparate impact, which is essential for ethical AI development.

---

### Q3.10: What does “model inversion” refer to in the context of AI privacy risk?

- **A.** Reversing a model’s optimization objective
- **B.** Recovering training data from model outputs
- **C.** Changing labels after training is complete
- **D.** A method of retraining a model on augmented data

> **Answer: B** — Model inversion is a privacy threat in which attackers use access to a model to reconstruct inputs from its outputs, posing serious risks to data confidentiality.

## Privacy and Data Protection Law Review Questions

### Q4.1: Under the GDPR, which legal basis is most often required for processing special category (sensitive) data?

- **A.** Explicit consent
- **B.** Performance of a contract
- **C.** Legitimate consent
- **D.** Implied consent

> **Answer: A** — Article 9 of the GDPR generally prohibits the processing of special category data unless an exception applies; the most common exception is explicit consent, which must be freely given, specific, and informed.

---

### Q4.2: Which of the following best describes*purpose limitation*in the context of AI?

- **A.** Collecting data from as many sources as possible
- **B.** Allowing reuse of data for any purpose deemed profitable
- **C.** Ensuring data is collected for a specific, explicit purpose and not reused incompatibly
- **D.** Using third-party datasets without reviewing their original purpose

> **Answer: C** — Purpose limitation requires that personal data be used only for the purpose for which it was collected, and not repurposed unless the new use is compatible or consent is reobtained.

---

### Q4.3: A Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA) is required when

- **A.** Any data is used for marketing
- **B.** A low-risk AI system is deployed
- **C.** A system is used internally by employees only
- **D.** Processing involves special category data and could pose high risks to rights and freedoms

> **Answer: D** — DPIAs are mandated under Article 35 of the GDPR when processing is likely to result in a high risk to individuals, including when AI systems process sensitive data.

---

### Q4.4: What role does a data controller play in the AI lifecycle?

- **A.** Manages the physical server infrastructure
- **B.** Develops algorithms for all third parties
- **C.** Determines the purpose and means of data processing
- **D.** Handles data breaches only

> **Answer: C** — A data controller is responsible for deciding why and how personal data is processed and carries the accountability for compliance with data protection laws.

---

### Q4.5: Which of the following would likely require heightened safeguards under privacy law?

- **A.** User preferences on website themes
- **B.** Facial recognition used for retail surveillance
- **C.** Email addresses used for newsletter signups
- **D.** A/B testing of homepage layout

> **Answer: B** — Facial recognition is a form of biometric data used for identification, which qualifies as special category data and requires strict controls under laws such as the GDPR.

---

### Q4.6: What is a common risk of AI systems inferring sensitive data?

- **A.** Low training cost
- **B.** Reduced model explainability
- **C.** Triggering legal obligations even if the data was not explicitly collected
- **D.** Inference accuracy cannot be evaluated

> **Answer: C** — When AI systems infer special category attributes (for example, religion, sexual orientation), this processing is still subject to the same legal protections and may require consent or an Article 9 exception.

---

### Q4.7: What is a key element of privacy by design in AI systems?

- **A.** Prioritizing model accuracy over user privacy
- **B.** Embedding privacy protections throughout the system lifecycle
- **C.** Outsourcing compliance to vendors
- **D.** Avoiding DPIAs unless requested by a regulator

> **Answer: B** — Privacy by design calls for integrating privacy considerations at every stage of development, from problem definition to deployment and monitoring.

---

### Q4.8: Which scenario best reflects the principle of data minimization?

- **A.** Collecting all available user data for future analysis
- **B.** Using anonymized transaction data limited to the current model’s purpose
- **C.** Archiving full user profiles for at least ten years
- **D.** Combining datasets from unrelated apps to improve personalization

> **Answer: B** — Data minimization refers to collecting and using only the information necessary for a specific task. Anonymized, purpose-limited data use is a textbook example.

---

### Q4.9: Which obligation must a controller meet when using third-party AI processors?

- **A.** Sign a DPA with required contractual terms under Article 28 GDPR.
- **B.** Include model logic in the user privacy notice.
- **C.** Allow unlimited use of training data by the vendor.
- **D.** Accept vendor liability for privacy compliance.

> **Answer: A** — Controllers must have contracts with processors that define the scope, safeguards, instructions, and responsibilities as outlined in Article 28 of the GDPR.

---

### Q4.10: Under GDPR Article 22, what must be provided when a decision based solely on automated processing significantly affects an individual?

- **A.** Access to the source code
- **B.** Unlimited data portability
- **C.** Meaningful human review and an option to contest the decision
- **D.** Permanent deletion of the data

> **Answer: C** — Article 22 of the GDPR requires that individuals subject to significant automated decisions have the right to obtain human intervention, express their point of view, and contest the outcome.

---

## Sectoral and Civil Laws Review Questions

### Q5.1: Which of the following best describes a potential copyright issue with AI training data?

- **A.** Using data from government websites
- **B.** Training on public domain materials
- **C.** Using copyrighted images without permission
- **D.** Using statistical summaries of datasets

> **Answer: C** — Using copyrighted images without permission may infringe on reproduction rights and does not automatically qualify as fair use.

---

### Q5.2: Under U.S. law, which of the following is a protected characteristic in employment discrimination cases involving AI tools?

- **A.** Internet usage history
- **B.** Political affiliation
- **C.** Education level
- **D.** Race

> **Answer: D** — Race is explicitly protected under Title VII of the Civil Rights Act. AI tools must be audited for disparate impact across such categories.

---

### Q5.3: What is a key principle behind the FTC’s approach to AI under Section 5 of the FTC Act?

- **A.** AI is not subject to consumer protection laws.
- **B.** Only AI used for profit is regulated.
- **C.** Undisclosed use of AI may be considered deceptive.
- **D.** AI disclosures must be approved by a federal judge.

> **Answer: C** — The FTC has warned that failing to disclose AI interactions may mislead reasonable consumers and violate deceptive practice standards.

---

### Q5.4: In product liability law, what type of defect occurs when a product’s original design is inherently unsafe?

- **A.** Marketing defect
- **B.** Manufacturing defect
- **C.** Design defect
- **D.** Distribution defect

> **Answer: C** — Design defects refer to flaws in the product’s intended specifications, regardless of how well it was manufactured.

---

### Q5.5: Which legal framework prohibits discrimination in loan decisions made by AI?

- **A.** Computer Fraud and Abuse Act (CFAA)
- **B.** Equal Credit Opportunity Act (ECOA)
- **C.** Digital Millennium Copyright Act (DMCA)
- **D.** Sherman Antitrust Act

> **Answer: B** — ECOA bars discriminatory loan and credit practices and applies to both human and algorithmic decision-making processes.

---

### Q5.6: A chatbot giving unlicensed medical advice without disclosure most likely violates which type of law?

- **A.** Trade secret law
- **B.** Employment law
- **C.** Consumer protection law
- **D.** Patent law

> **Answer: C** — Providing health advice without disclaimers or licenses can mislead consumers and trigger enforcement under UDAP (unfair, deceptive, or abusive practices) laws.

---

### Q5.7: Which of the following is not typically a valid defense against a product liability claim involving AI?

- **A.** The AI was too complex to predict.
- **B.** The product was used as intended.
- **C.** A safer design was available.
- **D.** The product caused foreseeable harm.

> **Answer: A** — Complexity is not a valid defense. Manufacturers are still responsible for designing reasonably safe products.

---

### Q5.8: Which regulatory body issued guidance clarifying employer obligations when using AI tools in hiring?

- **A.** Federal Reserve
- **B.** National Labor Relations Board
- **C.** Equal Employment Opportunity Commission (EEOC)
- **D.** U.S. Copyright Office

> **Answer: C** — The EEOC has explicitly stated that employers are responsible for ensuring that AI hiring tools do not discriminate.

---

### Q5.9: What is a key risk of using proxy variables in AI decision-making?

- **A.** They may have a disparate impact on protected groups.
- **B.** They can be more efficient than direct attributes.
- **C.** They reduce model complexity.
- **D.** They are required by most civil rights laws.

> **Answer: A** — Proxy variables (like postal code) can correlate with race or income, potentially causing discriminatory outcomes.

---

### Q5.10: Which of the following best describes the FTC’s stance on AI performance claims?

- **A.** They are allowed as long as results are plausible.
- **B.** They require substantiation like any other product claim.
- **C.** They are exempt from scrutiny if the AI is open source.
- **D.** They must be approved by a third-party certifier.

> **Answer: B** — AI performance claims must be supported by evidence and testing—just like any other advertising or product representation.

## The EU AI Act Review Questions

### Q6.1: Under the EU AI Act, which of the following is considered a prohibited AI practice?

- **A.** AI used in autonomous vehicles
- **B.** AI used in spam filtering
- **C.** Real-time biometric identification in public spaces (without exceptions)
- **D.** AI used in personalized shopping recommendations

> **Answer: C** — The EU AI Act bans real-time biometric identification in public spaces unless specific public safety exceptions apply. This practice is considered unacceptably risky.

---

### Q6.2: What is the key documentation requirement for high-risk AI systems under the EU AI Act?

- **A.** Patent registration
- **B.** Social impact assessment
- **C.** Technical documentation covering design, risk, and oversight
- **D.** Environmental footprint report

> **Answer: C** — High-risk AI systems must include detailed technical documentation that supports transparency, traceability, and accountability.

---

### Q6.3: Which type of organization is responsible for placing an AI system on the EU market under its own name?

- **A.** Importer
- **B.** Deployer
- **C.** Distributor
- **D.** Provider

> **Answer: D** — A provider develops and markets the AI system under its own name and is responsible for ensuring compliance.

---

### Q6.4: What is a key obligation for deployers of high-risk AI systems?

- **A.** CE marking
- **B.** Human oversight during use
- **C.** Notifying customs authorities
- **D.** Conducting environmental testing

> **Answer: B** — Deployers must ensure human oversight during operation to support safety and mitigate unintended consequences.

---

### Q6.5: Which of the following AI system categories is mostly exempt from mandatory regulation?

- **A.** GPAI with systemic risk
- **B.** Minimal-risk AI
- **C.** High-risk AI
- **D.** Limited-risk AI

> **Answer: B** — Minimal-risk AI systems, like grammar checkers and spam filters, are largely unregulated under the EU AI Act.

---

### Q6.6: What triggers enhanced requirements for a GPAI system?

- **A.** Use of open-source code
- **B.** Use in a single domain
- **C.** Systemic risk designation
- **D.** Training with European data

> **Answer: C** — GPAI systems deemed to pose systemic risk, due to scale or influence, face stricter obligations such as external audits and real-time monitoring.

---

### Q6.7: Importers of AI systems must ensure that the system

- **A.** Includes a software license
- **B.** Is CE marked and has passed conformity assessment
- **C.** Is developed in an EU member state
- **D.** Contains only anonymized data

> **Answer: B** — Importers are responsible for verifying that the AI system complies with EU conformity standards, including CE marking.

---

### Q6.8: What is the maximum financial penalty for prohibited AI practices?

- **A.** €1 million
- **B.** €7.5 million
- **C.** €15 million
- **D.** €35 million or 7% of global turnover

> **Answer: D** — The most serious violations, such as using prohibited AI, can incur penalties of up to €35 million or 7 percent of global revenue, whichever is greater.

---

### Q6.9: A distributor becomes legally liable under the EU AI Act if they

- **A.** Use AI internally
- **B.** Manufacture hardware for AI use
- **C.** Ignore known signs of non-compliance
- **D.** Provide AI training datasets

> **Answer: C** — Distributors must act if they become aware of non-compliance; failing to do so triggers liability.

---

### Q6.10: What is the purpose of a “model card” in the context of GPAI?

- **A.** It describes the hardware compatibility of the system.
- **B.** It registers the system with the European Patent Office.
- **C.** It summarizes technical specifications and known limitations.
- **D.** It licenses the model for public use.

> **Answer: C** — A model card is a transparency tool that describes an AI model’s design, training data, capabilities, and risks to inform responsible downstream use.

## AI Standards and Frameworks Review Questions

### Q7.1: What is the primary purpose of the OECD AI Principles?

- **A.** To create mandatory AI regulations for OECD countries
- **B.** To define technical testing protocols for AI safety
- **C.** To establish ethical guidelines for trustworthy AI
- **D.** To categorize AI systems by risk level

> **Answer: C** — The OECD AI Principles offer non-binding ethical guidelines designed to foster responsible, human-centered AI across member countries. They focus on values like transparency, fairness, and accountability.

---

### Q7.2: Which NIST AI RMF function is responsible for identifying the context and intended purpose of an AI system?

- **A.** Manage
- **B.** Measure
- **C.** Map
- **D.** Govern

> **Answer: C** — The Map function focuses on understanding the AI system’s design, context of use, stakeholder impacts, and associated risks.

---

### Q7.3: What distinguishes NIST ARIA from the NIST AI RMF?

- **A.** ARIA addresses regulatory compliance, while RMF does not
- **B.** ARIA focuses on assurance methods and testing techniques
- **C.** ARIA is a certification framework
- **D.** ARIA replaces the RMF in high-risk sectors

> **Answer: B** — NIST ARIA complements the AI RMF by providing practical tools, metrics, and methodologies to test and evaluate AI systems for safety and trustworthiness.

---

### Q7.4: What is a defining feature of ISO/IEC 42001?

- **A.** It is a privacy-focused AI framework
- **B.** It standardizes AI ethics assessments
- **C.** It offers a certifiable AI management system
- **D.** It provides a taxonomy of AI capabilities

> **Answer: C** — ISO/IEC 42001 is the first AI-specific management system standard that organizations can certify against, similar to ISO/IEC 27001 for information security management.

---

### Q7.5: What is the purpose of ISO/IEC 22989?

- **A.** To regulate foundation models
- **B.** To define global AI enforcement mechanisms
- **C.** To standardize AI terminology and concepts
- **D.** To guide AI procurement strategies

> **Answer: C** — ISO/IEC 22989 offers foundational definitions for AI systems, capabilities, and lifecycle stages, facilitating consistency across industries.

---

### Q7.6: Which G7 initiative provides guidance on AI governance?

- **A.** Hiroshima Process
- **B.** Kyoto Protocol
- **C.** Tokyo AI Alignment Act
- **D.** OECD Code of Conduct

> **Answer: A** — The G7 Hiroshima Process includes a voluntary code of conduct aimed at developers of AI and foundation models to promote responsible innovation.

---

### Q7.7: The IEEE 7000 series focuses on which aspect of AI systems?

- **A.** Model compression and optimization
- **B.** Ethical alignment during system design
- **C.** High-frequency trading algorithms
- **D.** National security applications of AI

> **Answer: B** — The IEEE 7000 series supports embedding ethical considerations directly into AI system design and engineering processes.

---

### Q7.8: Which of the following is a key trustworthiness characteristic emphasized by NIST ARIA?

- **A.** Profitability
- **B.** Interpretability
- **C.** Processing speed
- **D.** Licensing model

> **Answer: B** — NIST ARIA supports measurement and assurance techniques for trustworthiness traits, including interpretability, robustness, and fairness.

---

### Q7.9: What distinguishes ISO/IEC 23894 from ISO/IEC 42001?

- **A.** 23894 is focused on risk management, while 42001 is a management system
- **B.** 23894 defines training standards for AI teams
- **C.** 23894 only applies to AI in manufacturing
- **D.** 23894 provides legal enforcement guidance

> **Answer: A** — ISO/IEC 23894 provides detailed guidance on AI risk management processes, while ISO/IEC 42001 defines a complete management system for AI.

---

### Q7.10: Why is UNESCO’s AI recommendation particularly influential in the global south?

- **A.** It provides hardware specifications for AI systems
- **B.** It offers a prescriptive legal framework
- **C.** It aligns with principles of cultural inclusion and human rights
- **D.** It enforces AI bans on specific use cases

> **Answer: C** — UNESCO’s recommendation focuses on equity, sustainability, and cultural diversity, making it a practical and values-driven resource in countries without detailed AI regulations.

- **B.** To reduce storage costs
- **C.** To enable traceability and support governance
- **D.** To comply with open-source licensing requirements
