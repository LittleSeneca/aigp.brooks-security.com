# South Korea AI Basic Act — Key Provisions

> **Status:** In force (as of September 2026). Current text is Act No. 20676 as amended by Act No. 21311 (20 Jan 2026). **Effective:** 22 January 2026 (the digital medical device item followed on 24 Jan 2026; a few 2026 amendments on 21 Jul 2026). **Regulator:** Ministry of Science and ICT (MSIT). **Grace period:** MSIT policy, not statute: at least one year before fact-finding and fines, except in serious cases.

---

## 1. Overview

- This is South Korea's overarching AI statute. Its Korean title (인공지능 발전과 신뢰 기반 조성 등에 관한 기본법) is usually translated as the **Framework Act on the Development of Artificial Intelligence and Establishment of Trust**. The word 기본법 literally means "basic act", which is why it is also called the **AI Basic Act** or **AI Framework Act**. The BOK v2.1 calls it "the South Korean AI Basic Law".
- As a framework act, it does two jobs:
  1. It builds the state's AI promotion machinery: a national plan, a presidential committee, institutes, and support for data, SMEs, startups and clusters.
  2. It adds a thin layer of trust obligations in Chapter 4: transparency, safety duties for very large systems, duties for high-impact AI, and a domestic representative for large foreign operators.
- **Purpose (Art. 1):** to protect human rights and dignity and improve quality of life, while strengthening national competitiveness.
- **The rules sit in three layers:**
  1. The **Act** sets principles and duties.
  2. The **Enforcement Decree** (a Presidential Decree) supplies the thresholds and procedures.
  3. **MSIT notices and guidelines** fill in details, such as how to count training compute.
- **Compared with the EU AI Act**, it puts promotion first and regulates lightly. It has no banned practices and no conformity assessment. Its impact assessment is best-efforts only, and its administrative fines are capped at KRW 30 million.

## 3. Scope and structure

| Element | Rule | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Extraterritorial reach** | Applies to conduct abroad that affects the Korean market or users in Korea | Art. 4(1) |
| **Exclusion** | AI developed and used *solely* for national defense or national security, as specified by decree. The decree covers functions designated by the Minister of National Defense, the Director of the National Intelligence Service or the Commissioner General of the National Police Agency | Art. 4(2); Decree Art. 2 |
| **Relationship with other laws** | The Act applies unless another law has special AI provisions, and future AI laws must fit its purposes | Art. 5 |
| **Who is regulated** | "AI business operators": corporations, organizations, individuals **and government bodies** engaged in the AI industry | Art. 2(7) |

| **Explanation principle** | Affected persons should receive a clear, meaningful explanation of the key criteria and principles behind AI outcomes, as far as is technically and reasonably possible | Art. 3(2) |

## 4. Key definitions (Art. 2)

| Term | Meaning (paraphrased) |
|---|---|
| **Artificial intelligence** | An electronic implementation of human intellectual abilities such as learning, reasoning, perception, judgment and language understanding |
| **AI system** | An AI-based system that, with varying autonomy and adaptability, infers outputs such as predictions, recommendations and decisions that affect real or virtual environments for given objectives. This is close to the OECD and EU wording |
| **High-impact AI** | An AI system that may significantly affect, or pose risks to, life, physical safety or fundamental rights **and** is used in one of the listed domains (Section 5.1) |
| **Generative AI** | An AI system that produces text, sound, images, video or other outputs by imitating the structure and characteristics of its input data |
| **AI business operator** | The umbrella term, with two types: an **AI developer** develops and provides AI, and an **AI-using business operator** provides AI products or services using AI supplied by a developer. The English explorer translation calls the second type "AI service provider" |
| **User** | Anyone who receives AI products or services |
| **Affected person** | Anyone whose life, physical safety or fundamental rights are significantly affected by AI products or services |

## 5. Risk classification: which categories trigger which duties

Korea has no four-tier pyramid. Duties attach to **categories** of AI, and one system can fall into several. A generative model used to screen loan applicants, for example, is both generative and high-impact.

| Category | How it is defined | Main duties |
|---|---|---|
| Any AI product or service | — | Review in advance whether it is high-impact (Art. 33(1)) |
| **Generative AI** | Art. 2(5) | Advance notice to users (Art. 31(1)); label the outputs (Art. 31(2)); give clear notice for realistic synthetic sound, images or video (Art. 31(3)) |
| **High-impact AI** | Art. 2(4): listed domain + significant risk | Advance notice (Art. 31(1)); the six Art. 34 measures; a best-efforts impact assessment (Art. 35); strive for voluntary verification or certification (Art. 30(3)) |
| **"High-performance" AI** (a commentators' label) | Art. 32 + Decree Art. 24: at least 10^26 FLOPs **and** state-of-the-art **and** wide-ranging, significant risk | Identify, assess and mitigate risks across the lifecycle; run an incident monitoring and response system; submit the results to MSIT |
| **Prohibited AI** | **None**: the Act has no list of banned practices | — |
| **Excluded AI** | AI used solely for defense or national security | Outside the Act |

### 5.1 High-impact domains (Art. 2(4)(a)-(k))

| Item | Domain |
|---|---|
| (a) | Energy supply |
| (b) | Drinking water production processes |
| (c) | Provision and operation of the health care system |
| (d) | Development and use of medical devices and digital medical devices |
| (e) | Safe management and operation of nuclear materials and facilities |
| (f) | Analysis and use of biometric information (face, fingerprints, iris, palm veins) for criminal investigation or arrest |
| (g) | Judgments or evaluations that significantly affect a person's rights or obligations, such as **hiring and loan screening** |
| (h) | Core operation and management of transport vehicles, facilities and systems |
| (i) | Decisions by the state, local governments or public institutions that affect the public, such as eligibility checks or charges for public services |
| (j) | Student assessment in **early childhood, elementary and secondary** education |
| (k) | Other areas added by Presidential Decree. The current decree adds none |

- **Two-part test:** being in a listed domain is necessary but not enough. The system must also be capable of significantly affecting life, safety or fundamental rights.
- **Compared with EU Annex III:** the lists overlap heavily: critical infrastructure, biometrics in policing, employment, credit, public services and education. But Korea's education item covers only student assessment up to secondary school, and its list sits in the statute itself, with additions by decree.

### 5.2 Confirming high-impact status (Art. 33; Decree Arts. 25-26)

- The operator **must review status in advance** and **may ask MSIT to confirm** it.
- A confirmation request includes:
  - a summary of the product or service;
  - an overview of the training data;
  - material showing how the system is used and what results it produces;
  - any other supporting documents.
- MSIT weighs:
  - whether the system is used in a listed domain;
  - the impact, severity, frequency and domain-specific features of its risks;
  - the operator's own review;
  - any advice from the expert committee.
- **Timing:**
  - MSIT answers **within 30 days** and may extend once by up to 30 days, giving written reasons.
  - An operator that disagrees can ask for reconfirmation within 10 days.
  - MSIT answers a reconfirmation request within 30 days, after consulting the expert committee.
- The expert committee has 50 or more members appointed by MSIT, serving two-year terms renewable once.
- MSIT may publish guidelines with criteria and examples of high-impact AI (Art. 33(3)).

## 6. Transparency and notification (Art. 31; Decree Art. 23)

| Duty | Applies to | How (Decree Art. 23) | Enforcement |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Advance notice** (Art. 31(1)) | Operators providing products or services that use high-impact **or** generative AI | Stated on the product or in contracts, manuals or terms; shown on the user's screen; posted where the product is provided; or another method MSIT approves | **Directly finable** (Art. 43(1)1) |
| **Output labeling** (Art. 31(2)) | Operators providing generative AI, or products or services that use it | A method humans can recognize, **or** a machine-readable method such as a watermark. With a machine-readable method, users must also be told at least once, by text or voice, that the output is AI-generated | Fact-finding, then a corrective order (Art. 40) |
| **Realistic synthetic content** (Art. 31(3)) | Sound, images, video and similar outputs that are hard to tell apart from real ones | A notice users can clearly recognize, by sight, by sound or through software, adapted to the age and the physical or social conditions of the main users. For artistic or creative works, the notice may be given in a way that does not hinder display or enjoyment | Fact-finding, then a corrective order (Art. 40) |

- **Exemptions (Decree Art. 23(4)):** all or part of these duties can be waived in three cases:
  - where AI use is obvious from the product name, the screen text or the outputs;
  - where the AI is used only for the operator's internal business;
  - in other cases MSIT designates by notice.
- **Compared with EU Art. 50:** the EU splits transparency duties between two roles, and they apply from 2 Aug 2026.
  - Providers must disclose AI interaction and mark synthetic outputs in machine-readable form.
  - Deployers must disclose emotion recognition, deepfakes and certain AI-generated public-interest text.
  - Korea instead puts all three duties on "AI business operators", and they have applied since 22 Jan 2026.

## 7. High-impact AI duties (Arts. 30, 34-35; Decree Arts. 27-28)

### 7.1 The six Art. 34(1) measures, mapped to AIGP themes

| # | Measure | AIGP theme | Decree detail |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Set up and run a **risk management plan** | Risk management | Post the key contents, such as the risk policy and organizational structure |
| 2 | Set up and carry out an **explanation plan**, covering final outputs, the key criteria used to reach them and an overview of the training data, as far as is technically feasible | Transparency, explainability, data governance | Post the key contents |
| 3 | Set up and run **user protection measures** | Consumer protection | Post the measures |
| 4 | **Human management and supervision** of the high-impact AI | Human oversight | Post the name and contact details of the person responsible |
| 5 | Prepare and keep **documents** showing the safety and trust measures taken | Documentation, record keeping | Keep them for **5 years**; electronic storage is allowed (Decree Art. 27(2)) |
| 6 | Any other measures the presidential committee resolves on | Catch-all | — |

- **Posting:** items 1-4 must be posted at the operator's premises or on its website, but trade secrets may be left out (Decree Art. 27(1)).
- **Guidance:** MSIT may publish detailed measures and recommend that operators follow them (Art. 34(2)).
- **Equivalence:** equivalent measures taken under other laws count as compliance (Art. 34(3); Decree Attached Table 1).
- **Voluntary certification:** operators "shall strive" to obtain verification and certification before providing high-impact AI, and public bodies should give priority to certified AI (Art. 30(3)-(4)).
- **SME support:** MSIT may help SMEs carry out the Art. 34 measures and Art. 35 assessments (Art. 17(3)).
- **What is missing compared with the EU:** Korea has none of the following:
  - a quality management system;
  - conformity assessment or CE marking;
  - a registration database;
  - data-quality requirements for training data (only an overview, in the explanation plan);
  - an automatic logging duty;
  - explicit post-market monitoring or serious-incident reporting for high-impact AI.

### 7.2 Impact assessment (Art. 35; Decree Art. 28)

- **Best efforts only:** operators providing high-impact AI "shall make efforts" to assess its impact on fundamental rights in advance. Skipping it carries no fine, and it is not a trigger for fact-finding under Art. 40.
- **Procurement incentive:** national institutions (the state, local governments and public institutions) that use high-impact AI must give priority to products or services that have been assessed (Art. 35(2)).
- **Contents (Decree Art. 28(1)):**
  - the individuals or groups affected;
  - the fundamental rights at stake;
  - social and economic impacts;
  - usage patterns;
  - evaluation indicators and calculation methods;
  - measures to prevent and mitigate risks and to recover losses;
  - an improvement plan where one is needed.
- **Who performs it:** the operator itself, or a third party it commissions (Decree Art. 28(2)).
- **Since 21 Jul 2026:** the assessment must reflect the characteristics of AI-vulnerable groups, in light of the product or service (Act No. 21311). Decree Art. 1-2 defines these groups; they include people with disabilities and people aged 65 or over. Decree Art. 28(1)1 was amended so that the identification of affected groups takes them into account.
- **Compared with the EU FRIA (Art. 27):** the EU assessment is mandatory, but only for certain deployers: bodies governed by public law, private entities providing public services, and deployers of credit-scoring or life and health insurance pricing systems.

## 8. Role differences: developers and AI-using operators

| Issue | Korea | EU AI Act |
|---|---|---|
| **Role labels** | Two types of AI business operator: developer and AI-using business operator | Provider, deployer, importer, distributor, authorized representative, product manufacturer |
| **Duty sets** | One shared set, which attaches to whoever *provides* the high-impact or generative AI product or service | Separate duty sets (e.g., providers under Art. 16, deployers under Art. 26) |
| **Downstream relief** | **Deemed compliance** (Decree Art. 27(3)): an AI-using operator takes a system from an operator that carried out measures 1-3 (risk management, explanation, user protection). If it makes no significant functional change, such as substantially altering the original purpose or use, it is treated as having carried out those measures | A deployer that substantially modifies a high-risk system, or changes its intended purpose, becomes its provider (Art. 25) |
| **What stays with the downstream operator** | The deeming rule does **not** cover measure 4 (human oversight) or measure 5 (documentation), and the advance-notice duty also remains | Deployer duties such as human oversight, monitoring, keeping logs and informing workers |
| **Information flow** | The AI-using operator may ask the developer for the materials it needs, and the developer must try to cooperate (Decree Art. 27(4)) | Providers must give deployers instructions for use (Art. 13) |
| **Public sector** | Government bodies are AI business operators too | Public authorities act as deployers, and the FRIA is mandatory for them |

## 9. "High-performance" AI safety duties (Art. 32; Decree Art. 24)

- **Scope test:** a system must meet **all three** criteria in Decree Art. 24(1):
  1. cumulative training compute of at least **10^26 floating-point operations**;
  2. it is built and run with the most advanced AI technology now in use (state of the art);
  3. its risks could have wide-ranging, significant effects on life, physical safety and fundamental rights.
- **Duties (Art. 32(1)-(2)):**
  - identify, assess and mitigate risks across the whole AI lifecycle;
  - build a risk management system that monitors and responds to AI safety incidents;
  - submit the results to MSIT.
- **Details:** MSIT sets the method for counting compute and issues detailed guidelines (Art. 32(3); Decree Art. 24(2)). The criteria are reviewed every three years (Decree Art. 33).
- **Compared with the EU:**
  - All EU GPAI models carry baseline duties: technical documentation, information for downstream providers, a copyright policy and a training-content summary.
  - A model is *presumed* to have systemic risk above **10^25 FLOPs** (Art. 51(2)). That adds evaluations, adversarial testing, incident reporting and cybersecurity duties.
  - Korea has no general GPAI tier. Its compute bar is higher, it adds two qualitative tests, and the duty attaches to AI *systems* rather than models.
- **Terminology:** "high-performance AI" is a label commentators use (Cooley, for example). The Act itself just refers to systems whose training compute meets the decree threshold.

## 10. Domestic representative (Art. 36; Decree Art. 29)

- **Who must appoint one:** an AI business operator with no address or place of business in Korea that meets **any one** of these thresholds:
  - prior-year total revenue of **KRW 1 trillion** or more;
  - prior-year AI-service revenue of **KRW 10 billion** or more;
  - an average of **1 million daily users in Korea** over the three months before the end of the prior year;
  - a past fine for ignoring an MSIT suspension or corrective order.
  - Revenue is converted to won at the prior year's average exchange rate.
- **How:** designate the representative in writing and report it to MSIT. The representative must have a Korean address or place of business.
- **Tasks:**
  - submit the Art. 32 safety results;
  - request high-impact confirmation under Art. 33;
  - support the Art. 34 measures, including checking that the required documents are current and accurate.
- **Liability:** violations by the representative are treated as the appointing operator's own (Art. 36(3)).
- **Penalty:** failing to appoint one is directly finable (Art. 43(1)2).
- **Compared with the EU:** the EU requires an authorized representative for *every* non-EU provider of high-risk systems (Art. 22) or GPAI models (Art. 54), with no revenue or user thresholds.

## 11. Enforcement and penalties (Arts. 40-43)

### 11.1 Fact-finding and orders (Art. 40)

- **Triggers:** MSIT finds or suspects a breach, or receives a report or complaint about one, of:
  - Art. 31(2)-(3) (labeling);
  - Art. 32(1)-(2) (high-performance safety);
  - Art. 34(1) (high-impact measures).
- **Powers:** MSIT can require data, and its officials can enter offices and inspect books, documents and other materials. The procedure follows the Framework Act on Administrative Investigations.
- **Outcome:** an order to stop or correct the violation.

### 11.2 Administrative fines (Art. 43)

| Finable failure | Maximum |
|---|---|
| No advance notice of high-impact or generative AI (Art. 31(1)) | KRW 30 million |
| No domestic representative (Art. 36(1)) | KRW 30 million |
| Ignoring a suspension or corrective order (Art. 40(3)) | KRW 30 million |

- MSIT imposes and collects the fines. Per-violation amounts are set in the Decree's Attached Table 2.
- KRW 30 million is roughly US$21,000-22,000.
- **Not directly finable:**
  - output labeling, the realistic-content notice, the high-performance safety duties and the high-impact measures. These all go through an Art. 40 order first;
  - the high-impact review (Art. 33) and the best-efforts impact assessment (Art. 35), which fall under neither Art. 40 nor Art. 43.
- **Criminal penalty (Art. 42):** up to 3 years' imprisonment or a KRW 30 million fine for committee members who leak or misuse confidential information. It does not target AI operators.
- **No private right of action:** the Act creates none, although a complaint can prompt MSIT fact-finding.

### 11.3 Grace period (policy, not statute)

- MSIT announced a grace period of **at least one year** from 22 Jan 2026. During it, fact-finding investigations (including corrective orders) and fines are generally deferred.
- The exception is serious cases, such as loss of life or human-rights violations.

### 11.4 The EU contrast

- **EU AI Act fines (Art. 99):**
  - up to **€35M or 7%** of worldwide annual turnover for prohibited practices;
  - up to **€15M or 3%** for most other obligations, including Art. 50 transparency;
  - up to **€7.5M or 1%** for supplying misleading information;
  - for SMEs, the lower of the two figures applies.
- **Enforcers:** the Commission fines GPAI providers separately. Enforcement is split between national market surveillance authorities and the AI Office (for GPAI), against Korea's single ministry.

## 13. Comparison with the EU AI Act

| Dimension | Korea AI Basic Act | EU AI Act |
|---|---|---|
| **Character** | Framework act: promotion plus light trust duties | Product-safety-style, risk-based regulation |
| **Risk structure** | Categories: high-impact, generative, high-compute ("high-performance") | Prohibited, high-risk, transparency (limited) risk, minimal risk, plus a GPAI tier |
| **Prohibited practices** | **None** | Art. 5 list, applying since 2 Feb 2025 |
| **How classification works** | The operator reviews in advance; MSIT confirms on request within 30 days | The provider classifies against Art. 6 and Annexes I/III, with the Art. 6(3) derogation documented |
| **High-risk / high-impact duties** | Six measures: risk plan, explanation plan, user protection, human oversight, documents, other | Arts. 9-15 requirements plus a QMS (Art. 17), conformity assessment, CE marking, registration and post-market monitoring |
| **Conformity assessment** | None; voluntary verification and certification is encouraged | Required before a system is placed on the market |
| **Impact assessment** | **Best efforts**, for operators providing high-impact AI; public buyers must prefer assessed products | FRIA **mandatory** for certain deployers (Art. 27) |
| **Documentation retention** | 5 years (Decree) | 10 years (Art. 18) |
| **Transparency** | Advance notice (high-impact and generative), output labeling, clear notice for realistic content; all on "AI business operators" | Art. 50: interaction disclosure and machine-readable marking (providers); deepfake disclosure (deployers) |
| **General-purpose / frontier** | Systems with at least 10^26 FLOPs that are also state-of-the-art and pose wide-ranging risk: lifecycle risk management, results to MSIT | All GPAI: documentation, copyright policy, training summary. Systemic risk presumed above 10^25 FLOPs: evaluations, incident reporting, cybersecurity |
| **Roles** | Developer and AI-using operator share one duty set; deemed-compliance rule | Separate provider, deployer, importer and distributor duties; the Art. 25 role switch |
| **Foreign operators** | Domestic representative only above the size thresholds | Authorized representative for all non-EU high-risk and GPAI providers |
| **Regulator** | **MSIT** (a single ministry) | National market surveillance authorities, the AI Office (GPAI) and the AI Board |
| **Maximum penalties** | **KRW 30 million**, and only for three failures | €35M/7%, €15M/3%, €7.5M/1% |
| **Territorial reach** | Conduct abroad that affects the Korean market or users | Providers placing systems on the EU market, and non-EU providers or deployers whose output is used in the EU |
| **Exclusions** | AI used solely for defense or national security | Military, defense and national security; research; personal use; some open-source systems |
| **Timing** | All at once on 22 Jan 2026, plus a policy grace period of at least one year | Staggered from 2025 to 2028. The Digital Omnibus (Reg. (EU) 2026/1744) moved Annex III high-risk duties from 2 Aug 2026 to 2 Dec 2027 and Annex I duties to 2 Aug 2028 |
| **Promotion** | Extensive: Basic Plan, institutes, data, clusters, SMEs, startups | Limited: regulatory sandboxes and SME measures |

## 14. Applying it: a compliance sequence for a multinational

1. **Scope:** list the AI products and services that reach Korean users or the Korean market (Art. 4(1)). Screen out only systems used *solely* for defense or national security.
2. **Classify each system:**
   - Is it generative?
   - Is it in a listed high-impact domain, and does it pose significant risk?
   - Was it trained with at least 10^26 FLOPs, and does it meet the two qualitative tests?
   - If high-impact status is unclear, ask MSIT to confirm.
3. **Representative:** test the three size thresholds. If any one is met, appoint a domestic representative in writing and report it to MSIT.
4. **Transparency:** give advance notice for high-impact and generative services, label generative outputs, and add clear notices for realistic synthetic media.
5. **High-impact systems:**
   - put the six measures in place;
   - post the summaries and the name of the person responsible for oversight;
   - keep records for 5 years;
   - run the best-efforts impact assessment (it helps in public procurement).
6. **Downstream operators:** document that no significant functional change was made, so you can rely on the deeming rule. Ask the developer for the materials you need. Keep your own oversight and records.
7. **Monitor:** watch MSIT notices and guidelines, decree amendments and the end of the grace period.

## Exam Angles

- **No prohibited practices.** Any option saying Korea "bans" a practice, such as social scoring or real-time biometric identification, is wrong. Korea regulates through notice, labeling and high-impact duties.
- **Fines are low and narrow:** a KRW 30 million cap, for only three failures. These are missing advance notice, missing domestic representative, and ignoring an MSIT order. Labeling and high-impact breaches go through investigation and a corrective order first. Contrast the EU's €35M/7% ceiling.
- **The impact assessment is best efforts.** It is not mandatory and not finable, and public procurement provides the incentive. The EU FRIA is mandatory, but only for certain deployers.
- **High-impact AI needs a listed domain plus significant risk.** The operator reviews it first, and MSIT confirms on request within 30 days. Watch for K-12-only student assessment, and remember that hiring, lending, policing biometrics, critical infrastructure, health and public-sector decisions are all covered.
- **"High-performance" AI means 10^26 FLOPs plus state of the art plus wide-ranging risk,** with results reported to MSIT. The EU presumes systemic risk for GPAI models at 10^25. Don't swap the numbers.
- **MSIT is the regulator.** The presidential council deliberates on policy but does not enforce. The privacy regulator (PIPC) is a distractor.
- **The grace period is at least one year and is MSIT policy, not statute,** with exceptions for serious harm. The obligations have applied since 22 Jan 2026.
- **Domestic representative:** any one threshold is enough (KRW 1 trillion revenue, KRW 10 billion AI-service revenue, 1 million daily Korean users, or a past fine for ignoring an order). The foreign operator answers for the representative's violations.
- **Roles:** both operator types share the duties. The deeming rule covers only measures 1-3, and only if the downstream operator makes no significant functional change. Human oversight and documentation stay with it.
- **Dates and reach:** promulgated 21 Jan 2025, in force 22 Jan 2026 (one year later), Decree No. 36053. It reaches conduct abroad that affects Korea, and excludes AI used solely for defense or national security.
